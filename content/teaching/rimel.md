## Introduction

In practice, developers are most often faced with the task of "maintaining" code, whether to understand it, adapt it, correct it, or integrate new code. With the use of generative AI, this issue becomes even more pressing (e.g., Google's DORA reports from 2024 and 2025).

This crucial stage in the software lifecycle requires a variety of skills, some of which will be covered in this course.

## Goal

The goal of this module is to give students a new perspective on "code" and, as a result, enable them to become better developers, project managers or architects depending on the questions raised.

To achieve this, the course's overall approach will be based on self-directed learning and knowledge sharing:

- Lectures aim to introduce students to tools and problems that differ from what they are used to
- Tutorials are designed to enable students to (i) ask questions, (ii) develop methods to answer those questions, and (iii) use tools to answer those questions, sometimes employing approaches that differ significantly from standard development practices. To that end, students are asked to write a chapter for a book on "Learning from Code". This approach is inspired by: https://github.com/delftswa2016/desosa2016/

## Targeted Audience

Final-year Engineering Students (Master's level)
